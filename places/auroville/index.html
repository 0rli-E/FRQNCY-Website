Place

Auroville

Tamil Nadu, India

A universal city in southern India, founded 1968 to embody human unity. Inspired by Sri Aurobindo and The Mother. Endorsed by UNESCO. An active experiment in voluntary, post-national community.

Visit →
The story

On 28 February 1968, schoolchildren from 124 nations placed soil from their countries into a marble urn at the centre of an empty plateau in Tamil Nadu. They were inaugurating Auroville — the City of Dawn — conceived by Mirra Alfassa (The Mother), Sri Aurobindo's collaborator, as 'a universal town where men and women of all countries are able to live in peace and progressive harmony, above all creeds, all politics and all nationalities.'

Today Auroville is home to roughly 3,000 residents from over 50 countries. The Matrimandir — the golden meditation sphere at its heart — anchors a city designed in concentric zones of culture, industry, residence, and international living. The town runs forestry projects that have reforested the entire plateau, has hundreds of small enterprises, and continues to wrestle, openly and sometimes painfully, with what self-governance actually means.

Endorsed by UNESCO in 1966 and again as recently as 2017, Auroville is one of the few sustained collective attempts of the 20th century to ask whether a different kind of human society is possible — and to keep building it.
