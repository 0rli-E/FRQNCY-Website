Place

The Monroe Institute

Faber, Virginia, USA

Founded 1971 in the Blue Ridge foothills of Virginia. The world's leading institute for the structured exploration of consciousness — the home of Hemi-Sync audio technology and the Gateway Voyage program.

Visit →
The story

Robert Monroe was a successful radio executive in the 1950s when, with no warning, he began having out-of-body experiences. Rather than seek a diagnosis, he kept a careful journal — and eventually, in 1971, published Journeys Out of the Body, the book that put the phrase into the language. Three years later he founded the institute that bears his name on a quiet Virginia farm, with a single mission: to make the exploration of consciousness teachable, reproducible, and non-religious.

The institute's core technology is Hemi-Sync — patented binaural audio that gently entrains brain hemispheres into synchrony, opening reliable access to states most people only stumble into. Around it Monroe and his collaborators built the Gateway Voyage, a six-day residential program that has now been completed by tens of thousands of people, including a long roster of scientists, military officers, and clergy from every major tradition.

The Monroe Institute's research has fed directly into the US government's Stargate Project on remote viewing, into clinical work on PTSD and end-of-life care, and into the quietly growing literature showing that the human nervous system is capable of substantially more than the consensus model assumes. The hot tubs are gone, the stained-glass meditation rooms remain. People arrive curious. They leave with a different sense of what a person is.
