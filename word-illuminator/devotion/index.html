Devotion

The vow underneath the practice. Love acting as discipline.

i.

Definitions

1.Profound dedication or commitment to a person, cause, or path.
Her devotion to the work showed up in the hours no one was watching.
2.Religious worship, prayer, or contemplative observance, especially when sustained over time.
The morning devotion took twenty minutes. The shape of the rest of the day depended on it.
3.Earnest love, loyalty, or attachment, especially when expressed through consistent action.
Devotion is what love calls itself when it has learned to keep its promises.
4.A short prayer, hymn, or contemplative reading used in regular spiritual practice.
She kept a thin book of devotions on the shelf above the kettle.
ii.

Etymology

  • From Latin devotio — a vowing, a consecrating, an act of solemn dedication.
  • From devovere — to vow, to dedicate by a vow, to consecrate.
  • Compound: de- (down, completely, away from oneself) + vovere (to vow, to pledge).

The Latin devovere carried more weight than the modern English softening suggests. In Roman religious practice, a devotio was not merely a feeling — it was a formal act of giving oneself over, sometimes literally: a Roman general could perform devotio by vowing his own life to the gods of the underworld in exchange for victory in battle. The original meaning is closer to complete vowing than to warm regard. To devote yourself was to set yourself down (the de-) so completely that you no longer belonged to yourself — you belonged to what you had vowed.

Earliest essence Complete vowing. Giving oneself over so wholly that the self stops being the centre.

v.

Deeper illumination

Devotion is what survives when motivation is gone. It is love with a long memory — not the feeling of caring, but the act of returning. The mistake is to think devotion is an intensity; it is closer to a frequency. The same return, the same offering, the same orientation. Quiet, patient, often invisible. Anyone can be devoted on the morning the project is exciting. Devotion is what the practitioner does on the morning it is not.

A question to reflect on What are you devoted to — measured not by what you say, but by what you keep returning to?
