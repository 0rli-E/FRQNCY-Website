Discernment

The faculty that separates. Sees what is from what merely appears.

i.

Definitions

1.The ability to judge well; sound judgement that recognises subtle distinctions.
She read the room with the discernment of someone who had spent years listening before speaking.
2.The act of perceiving or distinguishing something not immediately obvious.
Discernment of the underlying motive took longer than the meeting allowed.
3.In contemplative traditions, the capacity to distinguish between true and false guidance, between authentic and counterfeit calling.
The Jesuit practice of discernment of spirits asks which inner movements draw you toward life, and which subtly away from it.
4.Refined taste or appreciation; the trained capacity to recognise quality.
Her discernment in books was earned by reading badly for a decade before reading well.
ii.

Etymology

  • From Latin discernere — to separate, to distinguish.
  • Compound: dis- (apart, asunder) + cernere (to sift, to separate, to perceive).
  • Indo-European root *krei- — to sieve, to discriminate, to distinguish — also the source of crisis, critic, and certain.

The image hidden in the word is agricultural: a sieve. The grain shaken until the chaff falls through and what remains is what was looked for. Discernment is not invention or judgement-from-above. It is the patient, repeated act of sifting — letting the not-it fall away until the it stays in the hand. The same root that gives us crisis is the moment when the sieving must happen; critic is the one who can do the sieving. To discern is to do the work of separation that distinguishes signal from noise — and to know which is which.

Earliest essence The seeing that sorts. The patient sieve that keeps what is real.
iii.

Synonyms & antonyms

Synonyms
Perception · Judgement · Insight · Acumen · Clarity · Sagacity
Antonyms
Blindness · Confusion · Indiscriminacy · Naïveté · Credulity

v.

Deeper illumination

Discernment is not skepticism. The skeptic refuses; the discerning sift. One closes the gate; the other reads the grain. To live with discernment is to be willing to receive without becoming gullible, to evaluate without becoming bitter, to choose without needing to denounce what was not chosen. It is the only faculty that lets the world keep arriving and still allows you to know what is yours.

A question to reflect on What in your life are you currently failing to sift — letting in what is not yours, or letting fall away what is?
