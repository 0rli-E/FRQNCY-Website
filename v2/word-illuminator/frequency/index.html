Word Illumination · 03

Frequency

Both wave and signature. The shape of what something is, vibrating.

i.

Definitions

1.The rate at which something occurs over a particular period; the number of repetitions in a given interval.
She kept a journal of the frequency of her own restlessness, looking for the pattern.
2.The number of cycles per unit of time of a wave or oscillation, measured in hertz (Hz).
A radio frequency of 100 MHz means a hundred million cycles per second.
3.The state of occurring often or repeatedly; commonness of occurrence.
The frequency of his visits made his eventual absence the louder note.
4.In contemplative usage, the characteristic vibrational quality of a person, place, or moment.
She walked into the room and the frequency shifted.
ii.

Etymology

  • From Latin frequentia — a crowding, an assembly, a multitude.
  • From frequens — often, repeated, occurring at close intervals.
  • The wave-physics meaning is recent: coined in scientific English in the late nineteenth century, formalised in 1930 with the unit hertz.

For most of the word's life, frequency meant simply how often. The crowd. The recurrence. The thing that kept happening. The leap into physics gave the word a second skeleton — frequency as a measurable property of waves — but the original sense was never replaced. Both meanings now coexist in everyday language: a frequency is what happens repeatedly, and it is also what something is when you measure its vibration. The two meanings touch at a single intuition: pattern.

Earliest essence That which keeps happening. The recurrence that reveals the shape.

v.

Deeper illumination

Every thing that is, vibrates. A frequency is not what something does — it is what something is, taken honestly. Atoms have one. Cities have one. A person has many, and they shift through the day. To live at your frequency is to stop borrowing other people's. The work is not to find a higher one. The work is to recognise the one that is already yours, and stop interrupting it.

A question to reflect on Whose frequency are you currently broadcasting — and is it yours?
