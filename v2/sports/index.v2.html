Sports

The most honest mirror we have. The body cannot lie about whether you trained.

We do not rise to the level of our expectations. We fall to the level of our training. Archilochus — 7th century BC
descend

Sport is one of the few remaining places where the body has the final word. What looks like competition is really practice with stakes — a way to find out what is true under pressure.

The opponent within one's own head is more formidable than the one across the net.W. Timothy Gallwey
The shape

Three frames most athletes pass through.

Acquisition. The first months and years. Awkward, over-thought. The body is being taught what it cannot yet do without supervision from the conscious mind.

Performance. The shape is there in practice but disappears under stakes. The work shifts to managing the nervous system around movement.

Release. The shape is automatic, the pressure is metabolised, and the player begins to enjoy the sport in a different way — less contest, more conversation.

A practice

Five small things that compound.

i
Move every day. Even fifteen minutes.

The body does not distinguish between training and rest the way the calendar does — it responds to the year-long average.

ii
Pick one skill, train it slow.

Five minutes of attention-full repetition of one basic movement, daily. Not a workout — a practice.

iii
Sleep eight, before midnight when you can.

Hormonal recovery is non-negotiable for adaptation. Training without it is depositing into a leaky account.

iv
Lift something heavy each week.

Once a week is enough to keep the strength curve from declining. The body's relationship with gravity must be maintained.

v
Compete, gently, often.

Pickup, pick-a-sides, club nights. The shape under stakes only grows under stakes.

The ball does not know you are tired.
The mat does not know what you meant to do.

Show up anyway.
