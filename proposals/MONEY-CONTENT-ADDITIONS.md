# Money — Content Additions (Maloney)

Concrete, paste-ready additions to FRQNCY's Money domain, drawn from Mike Maloney's monetary canon. Educational and neutral in voice — no goldbug framing, no doomerism — written so a curious reader can hold the ideas alongside Bitcoin, fiat, and consciousness-based work on money.

## 1. The Attributes of Money

Maloney's recurring framework distinguishes good money from poor money by a checklist of properties. He's repeated the list across the *Hidden Secrets of Money* series and his *Guide to Investing in Gold and Silver*. The canonical seven:

**Durable.** Money has to survive being passed around, stored, and forgotten in a drawer for a generation. A thing that rusts, rots, or evaporates can't carry value through time. Gold scores extremely high — it doesn't oxidise. Fiat banknotes are physically fragile but their digital ledgers are durable enough. Bitcoin's durability lives in the network, not the medium.

**Divisible.** Money has to break cleanly into the size of the transaction. A cow is not divisible; you can't pay for breakfast with a hoof. Gold is divisible by weight. Fiat is divisible to the cent. Bitcoin is divisible to eight decimal places (the satoshi).

**Fungible.** Every unit must be interchangeable with every other. One ounce of pure gold equals any other ounce of pure gold. One dollar equals one dollar. Bitcoin units are largely fungible at the protocol level, with caveats around tainted coins.

**Portable.** Value has to move with its holder. Gold is portable in small amounts but heavy at scale — moving a million dollars in gold is a physical event. Fiat is portable physically and digitally. Bitcoin is the most portable money ever invented: a memorised seed phrase crosses any border.

**Verifiable.** A receiver must be able to confirm the money is real, on the spot, without trust. Gold can be assayed but not casually. Fiat depends on watermarks and ultimately the issuer's reputation. Bitcoin is verifiable by any node running the software in seconds.

**Scarce (or hard to produce).** The supply must be hard to expand. Gold is scarce by nature — roughly 1.5% new supply per year. Fiat is scarce only by policy, which can change overnight. Bitcoin is scarce by code: 21 million units, ever.

**Store of value.** The previous six combined produce the seventh: purchasing power that holds across time. Gold has held value over millennia. Fiat currencies, in Maloney's framing, lose roughly 95–99% of their purchasing power over a century. Bitcoin's record is too short to claim millennial reliability but its design targets this property explicitly.

A useful eighth, sometimes added: **medium of exchange and unit of account** — does the wider economy actually price things in it? Today fiat wins this category overwhelmingly; gold and Bitcoin don't.

## 2. Money vs Currency

Maloney's most pedagogically useful distinction. **Currency** is a medium of exchange — paper, coin, digital entry — that carries the other six attributes *except* store of value. **Money** is currency that *also* holds purchasing power across time. By this framework, every fiat currency in circulation today is technically a currency, not money. The US dollar lost its last formal link to gold in 1971; since then it has been a pure currency.

This isn't a doomsday claim — it's a definitional one. Currencies work fine for transactions. They just don't reliably carry savings across decades. Historically, every fiat currency that has existed has eventually returned to its intrinsic value of zero or near-zero, though "eventually" can mean centuries.

For a sovereign individual the practical question is which property you need at a given moment. For groceries, currency. For multi-decade savings, something with the store-of-value property — gold, productive assets, scarce digital money, or land. FRQNCY's editorial position: hold the framework lightly, test it against your own life, don't outsource the answer to any single teacher.

## 3. The Hidden Secrets of Money Series

Maloney's free documentary series, funded by GoldSilver.com. Ten episodes plus updates, viewed over 120 million times. A genuine open-access education in monetary history.

- **Episode 1 — Currency vs. Money.** The foundational distinction. Why most of what people call "money" today isn't money. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-1/
- **Episode 2 — Seven Stages of Empire.** How empires move from sound money through fiat to collapse, and where the US sits on the curve. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-2/
- **Episode 3 — From Dollar Crisis to Golden Opportunity.** The timeline of the dollar's decline as world reserve currency. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-3/
- **Episode 4 — The Biggest Scam in the History of Mankind (in 7 Easy Steps).** A walkthrough of how fractional-reserve banking creates currency from nothing. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-4/
- **Episode 5 — Where Does Money Come From?** The mechanics of central bank issuance, US Treasury bonds, and the loop that ties government debt to currency creation. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-5/
- **Episode 6 — The Rollercoaster Crash — Deflation First.** Why Maloney expects a deflationary shock before any final inflationary blow-off. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-6/
- **Episode 7 — Velocity & The Money Illusion.** How the speed of money explains why printing doesn't always produce immediate inflation — until it does. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-7/
- **Episode 8 — From Bitcoin to Hedera Hashgraph.** Maloney's exploration of blockchain and DLT as monetary technology. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-8/
- **Episode 9 — Fall of Empires: Rome vs USA.** Parallels between Roman currency debasement and contemporary US monetary policy. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-9/
- **Episode 10 — American Bread & Circus.** The most recent flagship instalment, on distraction, debt, and decline. https://goldsilver.com/hidden-secrets/episode-10/
