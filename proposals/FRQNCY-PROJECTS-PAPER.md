## The premise

FRQNCY is not only a curation layer. The graph is the substrate, but the network has to *produce* — otherwise it becomes another aggregator with an aesthetic. A handful of projects get incubated under the FRQNCY brand directly. A larger handful get lifted via the Launchpad (task #55). The rest get found by people who would never have found them otherwise.

This doc tracks the FRQNCY-internal pipeline. Launchpad submissions live elsewhere.

## Selection criteria

When deciding whether a project belongs *inside* FRQNCY (rather than on the Launchpad as an external project), three filters:

1. **Substrate-only**: would this make the *graph itself* stronger? A new bed, a new topic surface, a new way to navigate — those belong inside.
2. **Editorial control required**: does this need to be held in FRQNCY's voice from day one? A manifesto, a flagship retreat, the canonical newsletter — yes. A specific teacher's app — no, that's a partner project.
3. **Defensibility**: does FRQNCY's curation give it an unfair advantage no one else has? If yes, build it inside. If no, support it from the outside.

Projects that fail filter 1 but pass 2 and 3 still belong inside if they're load-bearing for the brand. The crypto stack is the canonical example — it doesn't *expand the graph*, but it has to be in FRQNCY's voice and FRQNCY has structural advantage.

## Project lifecycle inside FRQNCY

1. **Concept** — referenced in this doc and in the master roadmap. Author owns the next step.
2. **Sketch** — one-pager with what, why, who, when, and how to know it's working.
3. **Build** — implementation. Status tracked weekly.
4. **Ship** — live to network. The *first* version is always a draft of the second.
5. **Iterate or sunset** — projects don't get to drift. Either active investment or formal retirement.

A project sitting in concept for more than 90 days without movement either gets a sponsor, a redirect, or a kill notice. The roadmap is not a graveyard.
