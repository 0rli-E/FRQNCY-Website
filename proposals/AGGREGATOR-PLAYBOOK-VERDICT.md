# The Aggregator Playbook — what Gentlemensgram actually did, and why it stopped working in April
> Deep research into whether the borrowed-content growth model is copyable for FRQNCY. Answer: it was, for eight years, and Instagram closed it on 2026-04-30 — ninety-nine days ago. What replaces it is a model FRQNCY is already accidentally built for. 2026-08-07.

---

## 1 · Yes — they borrowed from day one. The founder says so himself.

**Filippo Pignatti Morano di Custoza** (@moranofilippo), born ~1998, Italian noble family — not the "French aristocrat" The Tab reported, which traces to a misattributed article about an unnamed person.

His own words on the early feed, from the only substantive profile of him:

> "When I was curating my Instagram content in the early days I would post about the watch so much. You know, **early pictures of Alain Delon** wearing one, or even **Muhammed Ali** with his big arms and this watch."

And the magazine's own description of the account: "a carefully thought out feed of images **found all over (from Pinterest to the furthest corners of Google search)**."

The receipts are better than testimony, though. **His Pinterest scraping library is public and dated.** Board "Gentlemensgram" created **19 May 2018**, 1,151 pins. "Gentlemensgram story" — a feeder board for Instagram Stories — **31 May 2018**. Then `GMG: Cars`, `GMG: People`, `GMG: Architecture` through 2019. ~4,850 pins total, sourced from Tumblr archives, **Getty Images**, GQ, Elle, Hodinkee, Town & Country, and Mario Testino's own website. Pin titles map exactly onto today's feed: Jimmy Stewart and Carole Lombard, a Godfather still, Diana with her sons (Getty), "FuckYeahAlainDelon."

**The original content came last, not first.** Tuxedo Society was founded in 2024 by Gabriele Bonini and Riccardo Capotosti — Filippo is a member who later got listed as a co-founder, not its originator. So the "Via @tuxedosociety" bio credit cannot predate 2024. The event photography is a recent veneer over six years of harvesting.

**So the sequence was: borrow for six years → build original later.** That is the exact inverse of what I advised you two messages ago, and you were right to push.

---

## 2 · And then Instagram killed it. 30 April 2026.

Instagram extended its originality penalty from Reels to **all post types**. Mosseri, verbatim:

> "If most of what you post to Instagram is someone else's content, your account is no longer going to be recommendable."
> "We're no longer gonna show your posts to people who don't follow your account proactively."

The mechanics, from Instagram's own [Original content guidelines](https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines):

- **Account-level**, not post-level. Judged on a **rolling 30-day window**. Threshold is "primarily" / "most" — no published percentage.
- Penalty is **removal from every recommendation surface** — Explore, feed recs, Discover. Existing followers still see you. New-audience discovery stops dead.
- Recovery is automatic: get the trailing 30 days back to majority-original and eligibility returns.
- **Crediting explicitly does not count.** Named as insufficient: "a border, watermark, subtitles, or **a credit in the captions**." Also: speed changes, minor crops, screenshots with the username visible, and **a different caption**.
- Their test: *"If someone could remove your contribution and the content would virtually be the same, it probably needs more of you in it."*

Note the brutal detail for the Gentlemensgram model specifically: **the editorial caption I praised two messages ago does not save you.** Transformation has to be *on the content* — voiceover, on-image text carrying new information, creative graphics, remix. A beautifully written caption under someone else's film still is, in Instagram's own words, still unoriginal.

Read the first row twice. Wonderful Places has **19.6 million followers and averages 1,900 likes** — one like per ten thousand followers. That is not an underperforming account, it is an archive with a number attached.

Gentlemensgram lost ~9,600 followers between 11 July and 5 August. It peaked and turned over. You'd be buying a ticket to a show that just ended.

**And the cruellest data point:** @dailyoverview does everything correctly — fully licensed satellite imagery, provider credited in 6 of 6 posts, journalism-grade writing — and posted the **steepest decline in the entire set**. Licensing protects you legally. It does nothing for you algorithmically, because licensed imagery you didn't shoot is still imagery you didn't shoot.

## 4 · Three accounts are still growing. Look at what they have in common.

**@edm (EDM.com) — 1.24M, +0.30%, the only clear grower.** Bio: *"Your trusted EDM media source 🗞️ The definitive voice of electronic dance music."* No submission hashtag, no "tag us to be featured." It positions as **press, not a fan page** — shoots festival floors under media accreditation, writes in an editorial voice, posts 5–8×/day, and funnels to an owned Laylo drop list.

**@fuckjerry — 16.6M, +0.12%, 0.58% like-rate (best in the set).** The account with the worst content-theft history in social media survived by becoming genuinely transformative. Memes that add commentary are a *protected* category under the new policy. Its revenue is a card game that has done **$50M+**.

**@thedodo — 13M, 5.07% ER.** Pivoted years ago to ~200 in-house videos a month. Every credit line now reads "Hosted and Produced by @[staff member]." Revenue is pet insurance, Scholastic books, Airbnb experiences.

The pattern: **original production, editorial framing, and revenue that doesn't depend on Instagram reach.**

## 5 · The legal question is settled too, separately

**Iantosca v. Elie Tahari.** A company reposted a photographer's image **with a caption crediting him**, without permission. The court rejected every defence and held plainly that **attribution is not a shield against infringement**. Crediting has never been a licence. It's now not an algorithm strategy either.

The hashtag pipelines (#earthpix, #wonderful_places) confer no rights whatsoever. Those accounts have simply never been sued.

## 6 · The good news: FRQNCY is already built the compliant way

This is the part worth sitting with. Everything in the plan already passes Instagram's originality test **by design**:

- **Orlando's voice reading Orlando's script over ESA/NASA footage** = original. His narration is the transformation; the cosmic footage is public-domain raw material he'd be adding to, not reposting.
- **The own-words pipeline** is literally Instagram's test written as an editorial process — you rebuild the idea in your own words rather than reproduce the expression. "Could someone remove your contribution and get the same thing?" No.
- **The film day** — his face, his location, his take — is unambiguously original.
- **Isha, Antonopoulos, Greenfield, LibriVox, the PD library** are licensed or free, and everything you do with them adds voiceover and karaoke captions on top.

You were never going to be an aggregator. You accidentally built the version that survives the thing that just killed the model you were about to copy.

**EDM.com is your closest working template**, and the framing transfers cleanly: FRQNCY is a *curation network* — that is a media-outlet posture, not a fan-page posture. Own the audience off-platform (the email list is already your stated metric), post at real volume, write with editorial authority.

## 7 · The system to actually copy

**Collab posts are the unlock, and they make the partnership strategy better.** Instagram explicitly recommends collabs, remix, and the branded-content label as the sanctioned way to feature third-party work — a Collab appears on **both grids**, shares engagement, and counts as content you had a role in creating.

Rewrite the outreach ask. Instead of *"may we clip your teaching with credit"* — which takes reach and is legally naked — it becomes:

> "We'd love to do this as an Instagram **Collab**, so it lands on your grid too and our audience becomes your audience. You approve before it goes live, and either of us can pull it any time."

That gives the teacher reach rather than taking it. It's a stronger ask, it's the abundant version, and it fits the existing outreach kit's closing line better than the old framing ever did.

**Stories are the free lane.** Pure curation — sharing another teacher's work with no transformation — goes to Stories and native reposts, which Instagram designates for exactly this and which **do not count against the 30-day ratio**. So the impulse to spotlight others has a home that costs nothing.

**Manage the ratio deliberately.** Assessment is rolling and majority-based. Keep the trailing 30 days majority-original — trivial, since almost everything you make is — and push anything borrowed into Stories.

**Build off-platform.** Every healthy account in the study is healthy because of something that isn't reposting: a $50M card game, pet insurance, books and gallery prints, agency retainers, an owned drop list. Your equivalent already exists and is already the metric: emails captured, then members.

---

## 8 · One unresolved thread worth chasing

Social Media Today reported that Instagram's update will "not impact a set of **publishers we've identified who have licensing agreements and/or explicit permissions from content creators**." **That language does not appear anywhere in Instagram's published documentation.** It implies an allowlist with no public application process, no criteria, and no confirmed member.

Treat it as unverified and don't build on it — but if the partnership pipeline ever produces a folder of written permissions from fifty teachers, that folder is exactly the evidence pack you'd bring to Meta's partnerships team. Worth logging permissions properly from day one for that reason alone.
