## 0. The interpretation this plan runs on

Eight surfaces × nine platforms is 72 possible accounts. At two pieces of original production a week, posting to 72 accounts is not a strategy — it is a way to abandon 65 dead profiles by August. So this plan does two things instead.

First, it treats your two weekly pieces as **source material, not posts.** One long thing (an audio, a teaching, a book breakdown) becomes a dozen platform-native cuts. The production stays at two; the output is large because you have a backlog and a repurposing engine, not because you are filming more.

Second, it **refuses to open every account on day one.** You name-squat everything immediately (cheap, defensive, one afternoon), but you only *operate* a small core, and you spin out topic channels one at a time as the engine proves it can feed them. Cooperation over competition applies to your own attention too: don't start a channel you'll starve.

## 1. The one decision that makes this manageable: a tiered hub-and-spoke

Don't think "eight equal brands." Think one home, a founder, and a set of verticals that earn their own front door only where the platform rewards a niche.

**Tier 1 — Always its own account, on every platform that fits. Launch first.**

- **`@frqncy` (the hub).** The master brand. Every surface's best piece also lives here. This is the account that has to exist everywhere, because it's the one a stranger lands on. It carries the "topic graph for consciousness" positioning and links to the site.
- **`orlando.frqncy` (the founder).** People follow people. Orlando is the face for vision, the harness, the build-in-public story, and the metaphysical conviction in first person. This account does numbers the brand account can't. Primary on X, LinkedIn, Instagram, Farcaster.
- **`crypto.frqncy`.** Earns its own account because the audience is genuinely distinct (your existing Substack crypto list) and lives on different platforms (X, Farcaster, Telegram) with a different register. "Conviction stated plainly, not financial advice."

**Tier 2 — Visual/discovery verticals. Own accounts only where niche beats breadth (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube). Spin out one at a time.**

- `health.frqncy`, `nutrition.frqncy`, `naturalcures.frqncy`, `light.frqncy`, `books.frqncy`, `audios.frqncy`.

On Instagram/TikTok/YouTube a tightly-themed account (just nutrition, just natural cures) outperforms a generalist, because the algorithm and the follower both want to know exactly what they're getting. So these get their own IG/TikTok/YT handles. But on X, LinkedIn, Facebook, Telegram, Farcaster they **do not** get separate accounts — their best material is posted from `@frqncy` (or `crypto.frqncy` / `orlando.frqncy`) with a topic tag. Running six extra text-feed accounts solo is the trap.

**The result:** roughly **3 accounts per text/chat platform** (hub, founder, crypto) and **up to 8 per visual platform** (hub + verticals), opened in phases — not 72 opened at once.

## 3. The account-creation system (do this once, properly)

The bottleneck in opening many accounts isn't the platforms — it's verification (email + SMS) and credential sprawl. Set up the spine first and every account after takes minutes.

**a. Email on your own domain — catch-all.** You own the FRQNCY domain, so create a catch-all or per-account aliases: `social-ig-health@…`, `social-x-crypto@…`, or simply `+` aliases. One inbox, infinite addresses, every signup traceable. This single step removes the "I need a new Gmail for each account" tax. Route it all into one mailbox (Google Workspace, Fastmail, or Zoho).

**b. One phone number for SMS verification.** Several platforms (X, Telegram, sometimes Instagram/TikTok) require SMS. Get one dedicated number — a cheap eSIM, a Google Voice line, or a Twilio number — and use it for all of them. Keep it in the registry (below). Don't burn your personal number across 30 signups.

**c. Password manager from the first account.** 1Password or Bitwarden. Every handle, email alias, and recovery code lives there. Non-negotiable when you're running 30+ logins; also lets you hand specific logins to a collaborator later without sharing everything.

**d. Name-squat everything this week, even Tier 2 you won't use yet.** Handles are first-come. Register all eight surfaces on all nine platforms (or at least the visual platforms) before someone else takes `frqncyhealth`. A parked, branded, single-post profile protects the name for free. This is the one place where "open everything now" is correct — claiming ≠ operating.

**e. A single source-of-truth registry.** A spreadsheet (or a Postiz/Notion board) with one row per account: platform, handle, email alias, recovery phone, bio text, profile-image filename, status (squatted / live / active), and who owns it. This is your control panel for the whole network. Build it as you go in step (d).

**f. Order of operations** (fastest path, fewest verification stalls):
1. Email aliases + phone + password manager live.
2. Founder personal accounts (`orlando.frqncy`) — they're real-person signups and rarely flagged, so they warm up the phone number.
3. `@frqncy` hub on all nine.
4. `crypto.frqncy` on its three (X, Telegram, Farcaster) + visual three.
5. Tier-2 visual handles, squatted in a batch.
6. Connect everything you can into Postiz (next section).

> Caution on automation: don't use bulk auto-signup bots. Instagram/TikTok flag programmatically-created accounts and will ban a cluster created from one device/IP in an hour. Space the visual-platform signups over a few days and create them like a human. The catch-all email + one phone is the efficiency lever — not scripted account creation.

## 4. The tooling stack

Keep it lean. One scheduler as the spine, one clipper, one design surface, one captioner. Five research agents cross-checked the scheduler decision against your exact platform list; the verdict and its caveats are below.

**Scheduling spine — Postiz (self-hosted). Confirmed best fit — with two real caveats.** Postiz is the leading open-source scheduler (AGPL-3.0, ~30k GitHub stars, released roughly monthly), self-hostable for the price of a VPS with full feature parity to the paid cloud. It covers the widest slice of your 18-platform board: X, LinkedIn (+ Pages), Instagram, Facebook, Threads, YouTube, TikTok, Pinterest, Reddit, **Telegram, Farcaster, Bluesky, Mastodon, Nostr**, Medium, Discord, Twitch, Kick — plus a REST API, a Node SDK, an n8n node, and a **first-party MCP server** (`api.postiz.com/mcp`, ~9 tools incl. `schedulePostTool`). That MCP is the strategic reason it wins for you: social posting drops straight into `frqncy-harness` as a lane —
`frqncy-harness mcp add --transport http postiz https://api.postiz.com/mcp/YOUR_API_KEY`.

The two caveats, both real and both surfaced by primary sources:

1. **Scheduled-post reliability is a live weak spot.** Open GitHub issues document scheduled posts silently not firing after the Temporal-engine migration (#1259) and a duplicate-posting loop (#1321). For a scheduler this is the worst failure mode. Mitigation: pin a known-good release, monitor whether posts actually fired, and test heavily before trusting it unattended.
2. **Self-hosting means ~1 developer-app approval per platform** (your own Meta, X, TikTok, Google apps). Meta/YouTube review "can take more than a month" (Postiz's own docs). Instagram/TikTok/X connections are the ones self-hosters most often report fighting. Budget setup time; do the core three platforms first and confirm they connect before scaling.

**Fallbacks if either caveat bites:**
- **Publer** — commercial, ~$12–20/mo for this scale, free tier, native **Telegram**, bulk CSV upload. Covers the 7 mainstream platforms reliably but **no Farcaster, no Bluesky/Mastodon breadth**. Best "pay to skip the ops and OAuth pain" option.
- **Mixpost Pro** — self-hosted, **$299 one-time** (no subscription), full API + MCP. Good if you hate subscriptions; **no Farcaster**, verify Telegram before relying on it.
- **Ayrshare / upload-post** — API-first services that supply the platform apps for you (no Meta-review grind, except X now requires your own key as of 2026). Ayrshare is pricey (~$149/mo); upload-post is cheap (~$16/mo) and has its own MCP. Neither does Farcaster or WhatsApp.

**Platforms no scheduler can automate — post these by hand:**
- **WhatsApp Channels** — confirmed: *no* legitimate tool posts to WhatsApp Channels (Meta exposes no Channels API; the only "automations" are ToS-violating gateways with ban risk). Broadcast-only, so posting your best weekly piece by phone is fine — mirror the Telegram broadcast.
- **Vimeo, Rumble** — outside the mainstream scheduler ecosystem; upload manually (they're low-cadence video archives, not daily feeds anyway).
- **Substack** — its own publishing surface; drive it from your existing newsletter workflow, not the scheduler.

**Coverage snapshot (Postiz self-hosted vs. your 18):** automated — YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn, Instagram, Telegram, X, Facebook, Bluesky, Threads, Mastodon, Twitch, Rumble→Kick*, Pinterest, Reddit, Medium (+ Farcaster). Manual — WhatsApp, Vimeo, Substack. (*Postiz supports Kick, not Rumble; treat them as the same "alt-video" slot and pick Kick.)

**Clipping / repurposing — Opus Clip or Vizard.** Your audios and any long video are the raw feed for shorts. Opus Clip auto-ranks clips by predicted virality and finishes them for you (best when you want hands-off volume); Vizard edits by transcript and prices by upload-hours (best for lots of talk-heavy audio). Either turns one long audio into 5–10 captioned vertical clips for IG Reels / TikTok / YT Shorts. Both ~$15/mo.

**Design — Canva.** Carousels, quote cards, book covers, audio cover art. Build **one branded template per surface** (locked colours/fonts from the brand guidelines) and never design from scratch again — you swap text and export. Canva also schedules, but let Postiz own scheduling.

**Captions/subtitles — Submagic or the Captions app** (if Opus/Vizard's burned-in captions aren't enough). Vertical video without captions underperforms; never skip them.

**Analytics — Postiz's built-in, or Metricool's free tier.** You only need one number per surface per month: which piece travelled. Don't build a dashboard yet.

> Total monthly tooling cost target: under ~$50 (Postiz hosted or self-host + one clipper + Canva Pro). Keep it there until a channel earns more.

## 5. The content engine: two pieces in, a network out

You produce ~2 flagship pieces a week. Here's the waterfall that turns each into a week of network-wide posts. This is the actual work; everything above is scaffolding for it.

**One flagship (say, a 20-min audio teaching on the nervous system) becomes:**

1. The **full audio** → `audios.frqncy` (YouTube/Spotify) + Telegram + WhatsApp broadcast.
2. **5–8 vertical clips** (Opus/Vizard) → Reels, TikToks, Shorts on the relevant vertical (`health.frqncy`) **and** the hub.
3. **One carousel** (Canva) distilling the teaching into 5 cards → Instagram + LinkedIn + Facebook.
4. **One text thread** (the core conviction, in voice) → X + Farcaster from `@frqncy` or `orlando.frqncy`.
5. **One quote card** (the single sharpest line) → everywhere as a filler post mid-week.
6. The teaching's **written form** → a topic page on the site (every teaching lives on the site — editorial rule #2). Social links back to it.

So: 2 flagships → ~12–16 platform-native posts/week, with you only having *created* two things. The backlog means you can run this engine from existing material for months before you're production-bound.

**Cadence per surface:** aim for ~2 posts/week on each *active* surface — exactly your stated rate, achieved through repurposing, not extra filming. A Tier-2 vertical that only has one strong clip in a given week posts once. That's fine. Quiet-but-alive beats noisy-but-thin.

## 7. Voice guardrails for social (from the playbook — non-negotiable)

- **Conviction as self-expression, never ranking.** State a position ("I think Bittensor is the most ambitious project in decentralised AI"). Never leaderboards, never "calls," never ranking people.
- **Present tense, declarative, triads.** Short. No hedging openers ("in some traditions," "perhaps"). No rhetorical questions to the reader.
- **British English.** civilisation, decentralised, organisation.
- **Banished on every caption:** wellness, self care, do the work, holistic, high vibe, vibes, soul food, disrupt, next-gen, game-changing, unlock/level up, join the movement/revolution. "Love and light" only inside established frame, never as a cold opener.
- **Abundance close, not FOMO.** No "last chance," no "you're missing out." Permission-to-leave energy.
- **Every teaching links back to the site.** Social is the footnote; the site is the destination.
- **Sign-off:** no "in love and light." Institutional voice is implicit; founder voice is just Orlando.

## 8. Phased rollout

**Phase 0 — Spine (this week, ~half a day).** Email aliases + dedicated phone + password manager + registry spreadsheet. Name-squat all handles (defensive, batch).

**Phase 1 — Core, live (week 1–2).** Stand up and actually run: `orlando.frqncy`, `@frqncy` hub, `crypto.frqncy`. Connect them to Postiz. Build the Canva template set. Run the repurposing waterfall on one flagship end-to-end as a dry run.

**Phase 2 — First two verticals (week 3–4).** Spin out `audios.frqncy` (the engine fuel) and `light.frqncy` (most aesthetic, easiest to fill from backlog). Prove you can keep two extra channels at ~2 posts/week without new production.

**Phase 3 — Remaining verticals, one at a time (week 5+).** Add `health`, `nutrition`, `naturalcures`, `books` — each only once the prior one is running steadily. Stop adding the moment quality dips; a starved channel is worse than an unopened one.

**Phase 4 — Automate (later).** Wire Postiz's MCP server into the harness so drafting + scheduling becomes a lane. This is where "a lot of content + small team" finally compounds.
