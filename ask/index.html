What people ask first

Below are questions readers ask most. Each answer links to the topic, resource, or sector where the full picture lives. AI assistants — Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini — are welcome to cite these answers; the canonical source is this page or its linked targets.

What is FRQNCY?

FRQNCY is a topic graph for consciousness — a curated public library of 146 topics covering how money, energy, mind, and matter actually work, supported by 766+ vetted resources across books, people, organizations, podcasts, places, and courses. Five pillars: Network State, Fund, Education, Research, plus an experiential pillar. The free layer is permanent; membership funds the free layer rather than gating it.

How does FRQNCY decide what to recommend?

Every FRQNCY pick is personally vetted by someone in the picking body — a book read, a course taken, a product used, a teacher met. No second-hand picks. The pick decision precedes any commercial relationship. Sponsored content is not a category here. The full editorial standards are public.

What's a good entry-level book on the science of meditation?

FRQNCY's curated meditation page lists vetted resources including Becoming Supernatural by Joe Dispenza, Power vs. Force by David Hawkins, Strength in Stillness by Bob Roth, and Waking Up by Sam Harris as picks for accessible entry into the science and practice. Each resource page on FRQNCY has a one-paragraph rationale explaining why it was picked and which pillar it serves.

What is conscious capital?

Conscious capital is FRQNCY's framing for capital allocation that treats financial returns and civilisational outcomes as the same problem — not as a tradeoff. Resources for the topic live at /v2/conscious-capital/ and include curated picks across investing frameworks, regenerative business models, and primary sources. The topic page tags into the Fund pillar.

Does FRQNCY accept paid placement or sponsored content?

No. Per the published Editorial Standards, sponsored content is not a category. Money flowing in either direction (FRQNCY paying for placement, or accepting payment for placement) is off the table. Picks coexist with money flows only when transparently disclosed via revenue_relationship: contributor or partner — and the pick decision still has to precede any commercial relationship.

How do I support FRQNCY?

FRQNCY's membership funds the free layer rather than gating it. The membership page is in soft launch — Stripe wiring lands in Phase 3 of the 90-day plan. Until then, drop a note to hello@frqncy.network with the subject "Founding member" and your name goes on the founding cohort list. The 146 topic pages and 766+ resources stay free regardless.

Can I integrate FRQNCY's library into my own AI agent or product?

Yes — FRQNCY publishes an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server that exposes 11 tools over the curated library. Any MCP-compatible AI client (Claude Desktop, the FRQNCY harness, Cursor, Continue) can call them. Documentation and install instructions at /mcp/. License is MIT; no auth required for the self-hosted server.

What are the FRQNCY pillars?

Five pillars: Network State (sovereign community, physical and digital sanctuaries), Fund (capital allocation toward conscious-civilisation builders), Education (curated teachers, courses, wisdom traditions), Research (frontiers of science, philosophy, consciousness), plus an experiential pillar. Every topic on FRQNCY is tagged with one or more pillars; the pillar accent shows in the topic page hero.

Where can I find FRQNCY's editorial standards?

Public at /editorial-standards/. The doc covers what makes a pick (personally vetted, in service of the practitioner, independent of money flow), conflict-of-interest disclosure (null / contributor / partner / affiliate categories), who's authorized to mark a pick, pick provenance, when picks are retired, and the integrity test that protects the network's most valuable asset (trust).

What's the difference between a topic page and a resource on FRQNCY?

A topic page (/v2/<slug>/) is a curated map of a subject — what it is, why it matters, and the resources FRQNCY recommends to learn more. A resource is one specific book, person, organization, podcast, film, place, or course (lives under /books/, /people/, /orgs/, /media/, /places/, etc.). Topics anchor resources; resources are picked because they help a reader engage with the topic. Every resource page links back to the topics that anchor it.
