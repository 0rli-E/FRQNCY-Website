# Manifesto Lines — quotable copy for any post slot

**Purpose.** A bank of ≤280-character lines that carry FRQNCY voice. Each one stands alone, drops into any X slot, and can be expanded into a LinkedIn post or quote-thread. Drawn from the editorial standards, the network-state reading list, the topic-graph thesis, and the founder voice on `/people/orlando/`.

**How to use.**
- Pick a line. Post it as-is on X (already char-counted). On LinkedIn, paste the line as the hook and write 3–5 paragraphs underneath that extend it.
- Don't combine lines into a single post — one assertion per post.
- Don't add hashtags unless replying to a specific search surface.
- Don't add an emoji unless the line itself is a list and the bullet is functional.

**Voice check before you post.** Present-tense, declarative, conviction not hype. No "unlock", "leverage", "synergy", "10x", "circle back", "low-hanging fruit", "best practices", "actionable insights". No spiritual cliches as direct self-description. No engagement bait.

**Char counts.** Every line is ≤280 including spaces. Links count as 23 chars on X; lines below don't carry links.

## On curation and the picks

1. The picks are the work. Everything else is packaging. *(43)*

2. A pick is a public endorsement, not a directory entry. Most things on FRQNCY are listed because they're part of the field; only a fraction are picks. *(146)*

3. No second-hand picks. "I heard it's good" doesn't qualify. Someone in the picking body has used the thing, read the book, met the person, or studied the practice. *(166)*

4. Sponsored content is not a category. If a piece was paid for, it isn't editorial — and FRQNCY doesn't publish it. There's no in-between. *(137)*

5. Money flowing the other direction (FRQNCY paying for placement) is off the table too. If we want to be on someone's site, we earn it. *(132)*

6. Curation is a moat that compounds. Each good pick is permission for the next one. Each bad pick is permission revoked. *(122)*

7. Picks are flat. There is no #1, no top-10, no leaderboard. Everything that meets the bar gets the same badge. *(113)*

8. Retire a pick when the reason for picking no longer holds. Keep the directory entry; remove the endorsement. We keep the history. We don't keep the lie. *(151)*

9. The integrity test: would FRQNCY pick this if there were no possibility of money ever flowing from the relationship? If yes, fine. If no, decline. *(146)*

10. Information is in abundance. Discernment is not. The work is the second one. *(82)*

## On the topic graph and the project

11. FRQNCY is a topic graph for consciousness. 146 maps of how money, energy, mind, and matter actually work. 766+ vetted resources. The free layer is permanent. *(160)*

12. A topic graph is a library that knows how its books relate. Most websites are a stack of pages. A graph is the shape of a field. *(133)*

13. The free layer is permanent. Membership funds the free layer; it does not gate it. This is the only model that makes the curation honest. *(143)*

14. Most "best-of" lists were written for a search engine. FRQNCY's are written for someone who plans to read the book. *(120)*

15. 146 topics is a constraint, not a budget. Each one earns its slot by being something a serious reader has to know. *(118)*

16. The taxonomy is the artifact. The pages are the receipts. *(58)*

17. A network you can join with a keystroke and leave with another is not yet a polity. The question is what the membership earns you and what it costs. *(149)*

## On conscious capital, the fund, the network state

18. Capital allocated by conviction beats capital allocated by index, eventually, in the categories that matter most. *(112)*

19. The cleanest fund is one whose picks would survive being made with no fee attached. Same standard as the editorial. Same test. *(127)*

20. A network state is a bet that the next coherent unit of belonging forms online before it lands on a map. The bet is contested. The reading is not. *(146)*

21. Network states recruit. The land they occupy was previously occupied by people who didn't opt in. The next decade will demand consistency on this. *(150)*

22. Religious orders are network states that have lasted a thousand years. They scale by attrition and inheritance, not growth. Read them before you build. *(154)*

## On the attention economy and the AI age

23. The algorithmic feed solves discovery by surfacing what most people clicked. A curated graph solves it by surfacing what one person, named, would vouch for. *(157)*

24. AI summaries make curation more valuable, not less. When everything can be paraphrased, the only premium is the choice of what to paraphrase. *(143)*

25. The pages an LLM cites are the pages a serious editor wrote. The substrate is changing; the standard isn't. *(110)*

26. Engagement is downstream of trust. Trust is downstream of receipts. FRQNCY is built receipts-first. *(101)*

27. No leaderboards, no follower counts, no engagement bait. The product strategy is intentionally counter-engagement. *(115)*

## On voice, conviction, hype

28. Conviction is "this is the best book on the science of consciousness." Hype is "the ULTIMATE guide" or "you NEED to read this." The work is the first. *(151)*

29. Cooperation over competition. FRQNCY does not pitch itself as the alternative to anything. It points to what it's for. *(123)*

30. Present-tense, declarative. We are building a thing, not promising one. The receipts are on the site. *(101)*
