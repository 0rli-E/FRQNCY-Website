# HARO Response Templates — Worked Examples

Five worked responses, one per FRQNCY beat. Each example is the full thing — what would be sent if the corresponding query came through HARO, Qwoted, SOS, or #JournoRequest tomorrow morning. Every claim about FRQNCY traces to a real page on `frqncy.network` (links verified against the current site tree, May 2026).

Use these as the reference shape. Don't paraphrase a worked answer onto a query it doesn't fit — write a fresh one each time, but stay inside this structure.

---

## The shape (recap from HARO-PIPELINE.md §5)

1. Restate the query in one line so the journalist knows you read it.
2. Substantive answer — 2–3 sentences, present-tense, declarative, no hedging.
3. Optional second observation — one sharp sentence.
4. Attribution block: name, role, FRQNCY Network qualifier, one-line credential, editorial-standards link.
5. Direct link to the FRQNCY page that backs the answer.
6. Offer to elaborate / send headshot / point to a specific resource.
7. Sign-off.

~150–200 words total. Never longer than that.

**Response:**

```
Subject: Re: avoiding bias in editorial selection at scale

Hi [Journalist],

Re: avoiding bias in curatorial selection.

The cleanest defense is to publish the criteria before applying them,
so the curation can be audited against its own stated standard.

provenance of every pick on the entity itself: who picked it, when,
and what "personally vetted" actually meant in that case. Bias
```

### Anti-patterns to avoid

- "I'd love to share my insights on…" — sycophantic; cut.
- "FRQNCY is on a mission to…" — bans the present-tense rule by sliding into aspirational voice; rewrite as "FRQNCY publishes / curates / makes."
- "We believe that…" — soft. Replace with the substantive claim itself.
- "In today's fast-paced world…" — cliche; delete.
- "Unlock," "leverage," "synergy," "transformative," "groundbreaking," "10x," "ultimate," "secret" — banned per `audits/seo/CONTEXT.md` §4.
- Anything that names a follower count, download number, or engagement metric — counter-strategy.

## When to deviate from these templates

- **Print-publication style guide differs.** Some outlets won't accept third-person attribution blocks; match their house style.
- **Journalist asks a follow-up question.** Reply in the same voice without re-pasting the full template — just the answer + sign-off.
- **The query has a tight word limit specified.** Cut the optional second observation; keep the attribution block.
- **The query is from a podcast or video producer rather than print.** Skip the URL block; offer a 5-minute call to discuss further.

In every case the voice rules and the brand-collision rule ("FRQNCY Network" not "FRQNCY") still bind.
