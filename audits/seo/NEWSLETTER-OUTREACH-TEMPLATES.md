# NEWSLETTER-OUTREACH-TEMPLATES — Phase 5.9

*Created: 2026-05-14. Three templates corresponding to three relationship states. All notes are short, personal, and FRQNCY-voice — present-tense, declarative, conviction not hype. No SEO-spam phrasing ("high-quality content", "synergies", "leverage your audience"). No swap mechanics. No mass-blast structure.*

These are scaffolds. Orlando rewrites the specifics of each opener before sending — the writer-specific lines are placeholders marked `{like-this}`. The voice anchor at `~/.frqncy-harness/voice-anchor.md` and `proposals/EDITORIAL-STANDARDS.md` govern the final wording.

Always sign off in Orlando's name, from the founder's address, not from "the FRQNCY team". Brand the network as **FRQNCY Network** or **frqncy.network** the first time it's named, per `MENTION-MONITORING.md`.

## Template A — "Genuine appreciation, soft suggestion"

**Use when:** the relationship is one-directional appreciation. The writer doesn't know FRQNCY yet, and the right move is to write the note you'd write to a writer whose work mattered to you anyway. The mention of FRQNCY is a footnote, not an ask.

**Length:** 150–250 words. Subject line: a phrase from the issue you're responding to, not "Hello from [thing]".

**Structure:**

1. Open with the specific issue, sentence, or framing that landed. Quote one line if it helps.
2. Name what it opened up in your own thinking or work.
3. Mention FRQNCY in a single sentence as something the writer might find adjacent — no link-trail, no follow-up ask.
4. Close. No PS, no "would love to chat". Let the note land.

**Draft:**

> Subject: {one specific phrase from their recent issue}
>
> Hi {first name},
>
> Your piece on {specific topic / specific sentence} stayed with me — the line where you wrote {short quote or close paraphrase} mapped onto something I've been working through for a while, and reading you make it explicit moved the conversation forward for me.
>
> {One or two sentences on what it opened, specifically. Not "great essay." Show the work.}
>
> I write at frqncy.network — a topic graph for consciousness, 146 topics, 766+ vetted resources, intentionally counter-engagement (no leaderboards, no follower counts, picks made by hand). Mentioning it because the corner of the internet you write to is one I think might find it interesting, but nothing more than that.
>
> Thanks for keeping the work in public.
>
> — Orlando
> frqncy.network · orlando@frqncy.network

**Notes for use:**

- The opener must be *specific*. If the placeholder reads like it could go to any of ten writers, it fails. Re-read the issue you're responding to, find the line, paste it in.
- No request. No "would love to chat". No "let me know if you'd like to feature it". The point is to write a real note. If the writer ever wants to do something, they will — without being asked.
- If the writer is in a sensitive editorial space (consciousness, contemplative practice), avoid spiritual cliches in the FRQNCY descriptor. "Topic graph for consciousness" is the dry, accurate frame; don't dress it up.

## Template B — "Specific topic overlap proposal"

**Use when:** FRQNCY has shipped (or can ship) a topic page that genuinely extends or complements the writer's work, and there's a real basis for a cross-published essay, a feature in their reading list, or a co-written piece. The ask is concrete, not vague.

**Length:** 250–350 words. Subject line: names the specific overlap, not "Collaboration?".

**Structure:**

1. Open with their work — a specific issue or framing you're responding to. (Same discipline as Template A.)
2. Name the FRQNCY page that extends it — with the actual URL.
3. Make one concrete proposal: guest essay we'd draft, a feature in their reading list, OR a co-written piece. Pick one. Don't bundle.
4. Say what we'd bring — a draft on a defined timeline, a topic-page commitment, a podcast slot.
5. Close with an out: "no offence taken if it's not a fit".

**Draft:**

> Subject: {specific overlap phrase, e.g., "On curation as a form of saying no — extending your Strategy of No thread"}
>
> Hi {first name},
>
> Your essay on {specific topic / specific sentence} — I've come back to it a few times. The way you framed {specific framing} mirrors something I've been building toward at FRQNCY Network for the last {N} months.
>
> The FRQNCY page on {topic} sits at {frqncy.network/{slug}/}. It's not a takedown of {their concept} or a competing version — it's an attempt to wire one specific implementation of it into a 146-topic graph of consciousness, money, and contemplative practice, with the curated picks (books, people, orgs) doing the work of the argument.
>
> Three concrete options, in case any of them lands:
>
> 1. **Guest essay.** I'd draft a piece for {their newsletter} on {specific angle that extends their thread} — 1500–2000 words, your edit, your byline rules. I can have a first draft in your inbox in {N} weeks.
> 2. **Reading-list mention.** If you run a "things I'm reading" or "recommended" section, the FRQNCY page above is the most-honest single link I can offer for {specific topic}. I'd write a one-line gloss you could lift verbatim.
> 3. **Co-written piece.** I draft, you edit, we co-publish — one runs on frqncy.network, the other on {their site}. Topic would be {specific].
>
> No offence taken if none of these fit your editorial line. The work is the relationship — the post-it I'd write either way is "your piece on {topic} moved my thinking, and the curated picks at {URL} are downstream of it."
>
> — Orlando
> frqncy.network · orlando@frqncy.network

**Notes for use:**

- The URL has to be live, well-formed, and editorially defensible at the moment the email goes out. If the topic page is half-built, hold the email.
- Pick **one** of the three options. Bundling looks like a transactional ask; picking one shows you've thought about what fits their newsletter's actual format.
- The "no offence taken" close is non-negotiable. The relationship is the goal; the placement is a side effect.

## Template C — "We featured you; here's where"

**Use when:** the writer already appears in FRQNCY's `/people/`, `/orgs/`, `/books/`, or `/media/` library. The relationship is implicitly already there — the work is to make it explicit and bilateral. This is the warmest possible opener and should be used for everyone in the library before any of the colder templates.

**Length:** 150–200 words. Subject line: "{your name} at frqncy.network" or "{your book/work} — a note from FRQNCY".

**Structure:**

1. Tell them the page exists. Cite the exact URL.
2. Briefly say what the entry is and why it's there.
3. Invite corrections — anything they'd add, change, or have us link to.
4. Offer a soft thank-you slot (podcast, round-up, /aligned/ feature) without making the email contingent on it.
5. Close warmly. Sign personally.

**Draft:**

> Subject: {their name or work} at frqncy.network
>
> Hi {first name},
>
> A short note. FRQNCY Network (frqncy.network) features {your work / your book / your newsletter} at {specific URL — e.g., frqncy.network/people/{slug}/ or /books/{slug}/}. The page sits inside our 146-topic graph of consciousness, money, mind, and matter; {your work} is picked under the {Pillar} pillar because {specific, honest reason — e.g., "your framing of clean money is one of the cleanest entries on the regenerative-finance shelf"}.
>
> The page is editorial, not paid — picks are made by hand against the standards at frqncy.network/about. I wanted you to know it's there.
>
> Two asks, neither contingent on the other:
>
> 1. If anything on the page is off — title, framing, missing recent work — tell me and I'll fix it within the week.
> 2. If you'd ever like a longer conversation, the FRQNCY Podcast is open; a future "things I keep returning to" round-up on the site is too.
>
> Thanks for the work.
>
> — Orlando
> frqncy.network · orlando@frqncy.network

**Notes for use:**

- This is the **warmest** of the three openers and the one to use first when the writer is in the library. Lead with the fact that the page exists.
- The "two asks, neither contingent" frame keeps the email from feeling like a setup. If the writer never replies on either, the page stays exactly as it is — that's the whole point of the editorial standard.
- If the writer's work is `revenue_relationship: contributor` or `partner`, that has to be disclosed on the page already (per Editorial Standards §2) before this email goes out. Don't send Template C to anyone whose listing isn't editorially clean.

## Banned phrases (do not use, in any template)

Per `~/.frqncy-harness/voice-anchor.md` and `CONTEXT.md` §4:

- "unlock", "leverage", "synergy", "10x", "circle back", "low-hanging fruit"
- "high-quality content", "high-DA backlink", "SEO opportunity", "audience growth"
- "would love to chat", "circle back next quarter", "let's connect"
- "the ULTIMATE guide", "you NEED to read this"
- "vibrations" (as direct self-description), "unlock your potential", "raise your frequency" (the latter is also a brand-collision risk)
- "we believe FRQNCY aims to", "FRQNCY can become" — always present-tense declarative

If a draft contains any of these, rewrite before sending.

## Tone calibration — three examples that pass, three that fail

**Pass.** "Your piece on the porous mind moved my thinking on the metaphysics question. The way you held both rigor and reverence in the same paragraph is rare and I'm grateful for it."

**Fail.** "Loved your latest! Would love to explore how we can leverage our shared audience for a mutual win."

**Pass.** "FRQNCY Network features your book on the conscious-capital topic page at frqncy.network/conscious-capital/. The pick is editorial — no money flows from this. I wanted you to know it's there."

**Fail.** "Your book is a perfect fit for our high-engagement audience of 50k+ consciousness-curious readers. Let's circle back on a cross-promo."

**Pass.** "If a guest essay on curation-as-saying-no fits your editorial line, I'd draft a piece in your inbox in three weeks. No offence taken if not."

**Fail.** "Open to a swap? You feature us, we feature you — easy win for both."

The pass examples are concrete, present-tense, declarative, and grounded in real work. The fail examples are abstract, transactional, and read like every other SEO pitch.
