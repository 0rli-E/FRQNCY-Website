## Wikipedia's actual standard

Wikipedia notability requires **significant coverage in multiple independent reliable sources**. Specifically:

- **Significant coverage** — sources address the subject directly and in depth, not in passing
- **Independent** — not authored by FRQNCY, not press releases, not the founder's own work
- **Reliable** — published by outlets with editorial oversight (newspapers, magazines, peer-reviewed journals, established books)
- **Multiple** — at minimum 3, ideally 5+

Articles for Creation reviewers apply this strictly. WP:NCORP (for organizations) is particularly stringent.

## Notability path — what needs to be true before submission

Wikipedia accepts the article when at least 3 of these conditions are met. Recommended target: 5+ before submitting.

### Hard qualifying events (any one is gold)

1. **Featured in a Wikipedia-allowable publication** — NYT, FT, Forbes, Atlantic, Wired, The Guardian, MIT Technology Review, IEEE Spectrum, Harvard Business Review. Even a single 800+ word feature article is usually enough.
2. **Subject of a podcast episode on a Wikipedia-allowable show** — Tim Ferriss, Lex Fridman, Knowledge Project, EconTalk, On Being, Long Now. The episode title/description must explicitly cover FRQNCY.
3. **Academic citation** — peer-reviewed paper, book chapter, or conference talk citing FRQNCY as a curation network, network state example, or AI-discoverability case study.
4. **Books citation** — a published book on curation, attention economy, network states, or conscious capital citing FRQNCY by name.
5. **Wikipedia-allowable industry analyst coverage** — Gartner, Forrester, IDC, CB Insights (rare for FRQNCY's space; unlikely path).

### Soft qualifying events (need 3-5 in combination)

6. **Coverage in respected niche publications** — Stratechery, The Generalist, Common Cog, Marker, Substack publications with verified independent editorial. Each one counts as ~1/3 of a hard source.
7. **Podcast appearances on Tier 2 shows** per `proposals/PODCAST-OUTREACH-PLAN.md` — each adds a half-source weight.
8. **Aggregator listings** — Y Combinator's Launch HN, Product Hunt's daily list, Indie Hackers' featured launches. Half-source weight each.
9. **Conference talks** — speaking at a credible event (Long Now, Network School, NIPS, ICA, etc.) generates conference-program citations that hold up.
10. **Wikidata entity** with statements that reference independent sources — this doesn't qualify for Wikipedia directly, but it makes the eventual article easier and gives the bot infrastructure something to chew on.

### What's expressly NOT enough

- Mentions in social media (X threads, Reddit posts, LinkedIn updates)
- The founder's own essays or interviews
- Press releases or vendor-paid placements
- Listings on directories without editorial selection
- Self-hosted documentation pages or repository READMEs
- Citations by other unverifiable websites
