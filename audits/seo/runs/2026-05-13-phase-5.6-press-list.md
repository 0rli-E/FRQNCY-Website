## The three pitches

**Pitch A — "The 146-topic curation network."**

> A founder built a 146-topic public library to do what algorithmic feeds can't: be wrong about what matters less often than the median.

Targets the attention-economy + culture + founder-profile beats. Frames FRQNCY as the working answer to the "algorithms flattened culture" thesis (Chayka, Warzel, Newton, Merchant). Goes out to writers whose existing thesis FRQNCY Network is a concrete instance of.

**Pitch B — "Membership funds the free layer."**

> Membership funds the free layer. It doesn't gate it. A curated public-mission library that didn't take VC and didn't run ads tests whether the economics of editorial actually work.

Targets the conscious-capital + business + economic-experiments beats. Frames FRQNCY's funding structure as a working specimen of the conscious-capital thesis applied to editorial economics. Leans on the published editorial standards as the structural constraint that distinguishes it from values-statement performance.

**Pitch C — "AI-citation as the next link economy."**

> Being cited by ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini is the new link economy. A curated public library built to be cite-friendly is a different kind of distribution bet than SEO ever was — and FRQNCY Network is the working example.

Targets the AI-impact-on-culture + platform-economics + open-web beats. Frames FRQNCY Network as a publisher built for the new substrate — MCP server, llms.txt, ai.txt, structured data, published editorial standards as credibility substrate. The widest tech-press appeal of the three pitches.
