# FRQNCY Network — Press Pitches

**Purpose.** Three distinct story pitches for journalist outreach. Each is a real angle, not a press release. Each maps to the journalist target list in `PRESS-LIST.md`.

**Voice constraints** (per `audits/seo/CONTEXT.md` §4 and `proposals/EDITORIAL-STANDARDS.md`):
- Present-tense, declarative.
- Conviction, not hype. No "unlock", "leverage", "synergy", "10x".
- No spiritual cliches as self-description.
- Always "FRQNCY Network" or "frqncy.network", never just "FRQNCY".
- Cooperation over competition — don't pitch as the alternative to anything. Point to what FRQNCY is for.

**Founder one-liner** (used in every pitch):

> Orlando Eisenreich, founder of FRQNCY Network (frqncy.network) — a curated public topic graph for consciousness covering 146 subjects, anchored to 766+ vetted resources, with editorial standards published openly.

## Pitch A — The 146-topic curation network

**Target writers:** Kyle Chayka (The New Yorker), Charlie Warzel (The Atlantic), Casey Newton (Platformer), Max Read (Read Max), Brian Merchant (Blood in the Machine), Polina Marinova Pompliano (The Profile), Will Storr (Substack), Olivia Goldhill (STAT).

**Why this pitch lives here:** these writers already publish on algorithmic flattening, attention economies, and the post-feed information environment. FRQNCY Network is a concrete case of the editorial-curation alternative they've described in theory.

### Headline-shaped framing

> A founder built a 146-topic public library to do what algorithmic feeds can't: be wrong about what matters less often than the median.

### Elevator (1 paragraph)

In 2026, a single founder published a curated public reference library across 146 subjects — from meditation to network states to regenerative finance to the neuroscience of attention — with editorial standards posted openly, every resource personally vetted, and no paid placement as a category. It runs on a membership model where money funds the free layer rather than gating it. FRQNCY Network is the structural counter to algorithmic feeds: the picks are the work, the editorial standards are public, and the reader gets the same library whether they pay or not. The question worth covering: does deep curation compound when distribution belongs to algorithms?

### Why now / why this writer / why FRQNCY

- **Why now.** The "AI slop on the internet" thesis (Warzel, Newton, Merchant), the "algorithms flattened culture" thesis (Chayka), and the "every newsletter is being absorbed into ChatGPT" thesis (Newton's April 2026 Nieman Lab piece) all describe the same problem. FRQNCY Network is one concrete answer being shipped now — not a manifesto, a working library at 757 indexed pages with structured data and published standards.
- **Why this writer.** [Customize to writer's most recent piece — e.g., for Newton: "Your April 2026 Nieman Lab interview about reformatting Platformer for the AI era articulates exactly the problem FRQNCY Network was built to address from the curation side. - **Why FRQNCY.** Editorial standards are public ([frqncy.network/editorial-standards/](https://frqncy.network/editorial-standards/)). No paid placement. Picks are tagged with the pillar (`Network State`, `Fund`, `Education`, `Research`, experiential) and rationale-paragraphs on every entry. ## Pitch B — Membership funds the free layer

**Target writers:** Anand Giridharadas (The Ink), Marc Gunther (Nonprofit Chronicles), Noah Smith (Noahpinion), Justin Murphy (Other Life), Ramez Naam (Substack), Packy McCormick (Not Boring), Karina Funk (as source-quote).

**Why this pitch lives here:** these writers cover the architecture of money-in-public-mission organizations. Some are skeptics of "stakeholder capitalism" theater (Giridharadas); some are operators of next-generation creator-and-curation economics (McCormick, Murphy); some are documenting the working models (Gunther). FRQNCY Network's funding model is a working specimen worth examining.

### Headline-shaped framing

> Membership funds the free layer. It doesn't gate it. A curated public-mission library that didn't take VC and didn't run ads tests whether the economics of editorial actually work.

### Elevator (1 paragraph)

Most "values-aligned" media businesses end up at one of two equilibria: paywall the good stuff, or sell the audience to advertisers and watch the standards drift toward what pays. FRQNCY Network is running a third structure: a permanent free layer of 146 curated topic pages and 766+ vetted resources, funded by paid membership that buys deeper participation — not deeper content. The free layer doesn't shrink as membership grows. There is no advertising. There is no paid placement. The editorial standards are published openly to bind the operator. The story worth covering is whether the economics work: can a curated reference library at this scale sustain itself on conviction-membership rather than gate-and-extract?

### Why now / why this writer / why FRQNCY

- **Why now.** The post-attention-economy correction is happening in public — Substack's revenue model, Ghost's open-source push, the patron-economy resurgence, the B-Corp expansion, the conscious-capital fund proliferation. FRQNCY Network is a working example of editorial structure designed against the gravity of paywall-or-ads from the first commit.
- **Why this writer.** [Customize — for Giridharadas: "Your Epstein Class series argues that the 'private wealth solving public problems' frame is the failure mode. FRQNCY Network is one attempt at the opposite structure: public goods funded by membership that buys participation, not access." For Gunther: "Your work on compassionate capitalism asked what businesses look like when service is the structural posture, not a marketing one. The 'membership funds the free layer' line is one operator's answer."]
- **Why FRQNCY.** Editorial standards are at [frqncy.network/editorial-standards/](https://frqncy.network/editorial-standards/), including the conflict-of-interest matrix that names the three relationship types (`null`, `contributor`, `partner`) and the disclosure rules. The picking protocol is published. The integrity test is stated: would FRQNCY pick this if no money could ever flow from the relationship?

## Pitch C — AI-citation as the next link economy

**Target writers:** Tyler Cowen (The Free Press / Marginal Revolution), Sangeet Paul Choudary (Platforms Substack), Anil Dash (anildash.com), Mike Solana (Pirate Wires), Casey Newton (Platformer), Mario Gabriele (The Generalist), Eric Newcomer (Newcomer), Antonio García Martínez (The Pull Request).

**Why this pitch lives here:** these writers cover platform economics, knowledge-economy restacking, the open web, and the structural shifts in how distribution works. Choudary's 2025 thesis (AI restacks the knowledge economy) is the parent argument. Cowen's 2026 generative-book release names the moment. FRQNCY Network is a working example of a publisher built for the new substrate.

### Headline-shaped framing

> Being cited by ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini is the new link economy. A curated public library built to be cite-friendly is a different kind of distribution bet than SEO ever was — and FRQNCY Network is the working example.

### Elevator (1 paragraph)

For 25 years, the link economy ran on Google's PageRank: the writer's job was to be linkable, and the link was the unit of distribution. In 2026, AI engines (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot) are doing increasingly more of the answering work that Google used to delegate, and the unit of distribution is becoming the citation. The website that ChatGPT cites for "what is meditation" is the one Anthropic's training data ingested as authoritative on the subject. FRQNCY Network is built explicitly for this substrate: structured data on every page, an MCP server (`frqncy-content`) that serves the 146 topics + 766 resources to any LLM that wants to ground, an `llms.txt` and `ai.txt` declaring authorial intent, and editorial standards published openly so the engines have a citable substrate of credibility. The story worth covering: what does it mean structurally when founders compete on AI-cite-ability instead of search-rank, and what is the publishing world going to look like once that flip is complete?

### Why now / why this writer / why FRQNCY

- **Why now.** Sangeet Paul Choudary published *Reshuffle: Who Wins When AI Restacks the Knowledge Economy* in 2025 — the parent thesis. Tyler Cowen's 2026 generative book (*The Marginal Revolution: Rise and Decline, and the Pending AI Revolution*) is itself structured as an AI-ingestible artifact. Anil Dash's March 2026 "Endgame for the Open Web" piece names the inflection point. The shift is happening in public, but no one's profiled a publisher that designed for it from the first commit.
- **Why this writer.** [Customize. For Choudary: "Your Reshuffle thesis is the structural argument; FRQNCY Network is one publisher's attempt to ship to the new substrate from a standing start." For Cowen: "Your generative book is itself an instance of the new format — FRQNCY's MCP server (frqncy.network/mcp/) is the equivalent infrastructure on the reference-library side." For Dash: "Your 'Endgame for the Open Web' piece names the moment. FRQNCY Network's bet is that the open web survives by being structurally citable, not by being defensively walled."]
- **Why FRQNCY.** The MCP server (`frqncy-content`) is public and serves the topic graph to any LLM. The `llms.txt` declares authorial intent. The `ai.txt` declares crawl posture. Every topic page has structured data (Article schema, BreadcrumbList, ItemList where appropriate). The Knowledge Graph entity briefs are in motion (per Phase 4.5). This is the working infrastructure of being cite-friendly, not the manifesto.
